    question: When and why were the stories of Saul and David written?
    short: Scholars read the books of Samuel as older sources gathered into the Deuteronomistic History, and some describe the story of David's rise as written to justify his taking the throne. When the sources were written is disputed.
    text: Finkelstein says it is widely accepted that 1 Samuel contains pre-Deuteronomistic material incorporated into the Deuteronomistic History. The summary of Coogan and Chapman's textbook says the authors of Samuel used independent sources that often duplicate or contradict one another, among them the Ark Narrative and the History of David's Rise, and the NABRE introduction lists independent blocks, including the Succession Narrative, shaped by an editor into the books as part of the Deuteronomistic History. Coogan and Chapman call the History of David's Rise a propagandistic piece meant to explain and justify David's ascent to the throne, and the Succession Narrative a hypothetical source explaining why Solomon became heir. Hayes says the story may be an apology for David but is also subtly critical of him, and that David's lament for Saul and Jonathan clears him of any part in Saul's death. Garsiel reports that some scholars have discerned an apologetic tendency in the cycle of David's rise and interpret it in various ways. On dates, Goldingay says we can only make informed guesses about how Samuel came into being; he reports an influential theory linking a first edition to the time of Josiah, in the late seventh century, and a suggestion that the Second Temple period was the key one, and says two centuries of research have produced no progress in agreement on Samuel's historical value. Garsiel says the book's historical value has become a subject of bitter disagreement, and argues that its author was close in time to David.

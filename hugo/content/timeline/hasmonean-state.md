  note: "Sources date Judea's independence from the Seleucids to 143–142 BCE, when Demetrius II recognized it (Stern, Encyclopaedia Judaica), to 141, when Simon declared it (Levine), or to 129 (Henze and Werline's chronology, which has Seleucid rule of Judea end in 142). The NABRE note puts the year 1 Maccabees gives for the lifting of “the yoke of the Gentiles” at March 142 to April 141 BCE; Britannica dates Simon's taking of the Akra and the decree in his honor to 142–141, and Henze and Werline make him high priest, commander and ethnarch in 140. Aristobulus I (104–103 BCE) was the first Hasmonean to take the title of king. Pompey captured Jerusalem in 63 BCE and Roman rule of Judea began, ending the Hasmonean state's independence; Hasmonean rule came to a close with the death of Antigonus in 37 BCE (Stern)."

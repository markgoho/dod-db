# Dating claims inventory

Research for [#78](https://github.com/markgoho/dod-db/issues/78), "Which dating claims do the Transcripts make?", a child of the map [#76](https://github.com/markgoho/dod-db/issues/76). Findings only; nothing here is a spec.

## Answer in brief

- The Transcripts make a lot of dating claims: 716 were extracted from 166 of the 189 Episodes, 435 about the composition of Scriptural Books and 281 about events. The 716 counts a claim once per Episode, so repeats across Episodes are included; merged, they come to 102 subjects.
- Almost all of them are critical dates. A traditional or apologetic **date** is stated in about 49 of the 716 (7%). About 123 (17%) state only a traditional author, setting or reading (Moses, Solomon, Daniel in Babylon, the apostle John), which implies a date without giving one. The remaining 544 (76%) give no traditional view at all.
- The archive can carry the critical side of a timeline from about 1208 BCE to 367 CE with few holes. It can carry a two-sided contrast for roughly twenty subjects (listed under "What the archive can support"). For everything else the traditional date would have to come from outside the show.
- Dan McClellan is the Speaker for 606 of the 716 claims. Guest Speakers account for 95, Dan Beecher for 15.

## Method and coverage

- **Source**: the 189 final Transcripts mapped in `data/processed-videos.json`. Episode Numbers come from that file's `episodeNumber`, not from filenames.
- **Excluded**: `-raw.txt` files, and three final-format files that are not Episodes in the JSON (`2025-12-29-shiny-happy-people.txt`, `2026-04-26-is-the-kjv-crap-sentences.txt`, `2026-04-26-is-the-kjv-crap-sentences-diagnostic.txt`).
- **Filter**: a broad dating-language pattern (BCE/CE, centuries, named periods, "written in", "composed", "dates to", authorship and pseudepigraphy terms, 3-4 digit numbers, key rulers and events) selected 3,614 of 37,599 speaker turns, about 2.2 MB of 10.7 MB.
- **Reading**: eight parallel readers each read one slice of those turns in full and recorded every dating claim with a verbatim quote. I merged and deduplicated the 716 rows into the two tables below.
- **Second pass for the traditional side**: the first filter had no terms for "tradition", "apologist", "supposed to", "purports", "inerrant", "creationist", "not historical" or "didn't write". A second filter on those terms found 673 further turns that also name a datable subject. I read only the 59 sentences in them that pair such a term with a subject and an authorship or dating word, not the 673 turns in full. None gave a traditional date. A few gave traditional authorship for subjects already in the tables (1 Peter E52, Luke-Acts E54, Psalms and Genesis E175, Revelation E156). No rows were added.
- **Verification**: every quote in the tables was checked by script as an exact substring of that Episode's final Transcript. Speaker and timestamp were taken from the Transcript line the quote sits in.
- **One row per subject**: each row gives the clearest quote and lists the other Episode Numbers that make a dating claim about the same subject. Where the show states both sides, the row carries one quote for each.
- **Date columns contain only what was said on the show.** Nothing was filled in from outside knowledge. "not stated" means no traditional view was voiced in the turns read.
- **Traditional column tags**: `[explicit]` means the show voiced a year, century, named period, age or duration for a traditional, apologetic, popular or early-date position. `[authorship/setting only]` means only a traditional author, a traditional reading, or the text's own narrative setting was stated.

### Not covered

- Turns with no dating keyword were not read. A claim phrased without any of the filter terms is missed. Readers found a few such turns while checking context (for example E75 00:19:44, "these events that didn't happen", and E67 00:28:26 on traditional authorship of Samuel).
- "No dated claim found" below means none was found by this search. It does not prove the subject is never dated on the show.
- Dates of manuscripts, translations (including the Septuagint), inscriptions as artifacts, church history after about 400 CE, and doctrine history were skipped unless tied to an event or book in the tables.
- The summary cells (traditional and critical columns) are my condensation of the extracted rows. The quotes were machine-verified, and every number in a summary cell was checked by script to occur in a cited Episode's Transcript. The wording around those numbers, and the "other Episodes" lists, were not individually re-read.
- The eight readers were model passes, not a human read. Their extraction was not double-read.
- No external scholarship was consulted. This file does not check whether the show's dates match the field.
- Speaker labels are model-inferred. Readers flagged Episodes 7, 144 and 145 as having turns labelled Dan Beecher that read like Dan McClellan; the Herod Antipas row quotes one of them.
- Transcripts are machine transcriptions. Numbers are quoted as they appear ("25, 2600 years ago" means 2,500-2,600).

## What the archive can support

### A one-sided critical timeline: yes

The critical side has dated anchors, most of them repeated across many Episodes, for:

- **Events**: Israel first attested c. 1208 BCE; Hebrew emerges 1100-1000; YHWH attested c. 850; fall of Samaria 722; Sennacherib 701; Josiah c. 640-609; deportations 597 and 587/586; Cyrus 539; Second Temple c. 519; Alexander 332; Antiochus IV 167-164; Hasmonean kingdom to 63 BCE; Herod's death 4 BCE; ministry of Jesus in the 30s CE; Paul in the 50s-60s; Temple destroyed 70 CE; Athanasius's canon list 367 CE.
- **Composition**: every major block has at least one critical date. That covers the Pentateuch and its strata, the Deuteronomistic History, Chronicles, the wisdom books, the three parts of Isaiah, Daniel, the four Gospels, Acts, the disputed and pastoral letters, the catholic epistles and Revelation.

Episode 187 ("Lay of the Land" segment) is a ready-made period chronology from the Bronze Age to 70 CE and is the single best source for the event spine.

## Gaps

### Scriptural Books with no dated composition claim found

- **Hebrew Bible**: Joshua (traditional author only), Ezra-Nehemiah (traditional author only), Lamentations (vague), Joel, Obadiah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Malachi. Amos and Micah are dated only as "8th-century prophets". Zechariah gets only "centuries before Jesus". Haggai gets only its internal date. Leviticus and Numbers are dated by stratum (Priestly, Holiness Code, Korah), never as books.
- **Deuterocanon and related**: Tobit, Judith, Wisdom of Solomon (attribution only), 3-4 Maccabees, 2 Esdras, Prayer of Manasseh. A mention check found these named in one to six Episodes each, none with a date.
- **New Testament**: no undisputed Pauline letter is given a year (Romans, 1-2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon). The show says only that Paul wrote them, that he was active in the 50s and early 60s, and that they are the earliest New Testament texts. Colossians, 2 Thessalonians and 1-3 John get authorship verdicts but no date.

### Events and periods thin or absent

- No traditional date is stated for Moses or Sinai apart from the Exodus, for the division of the kingdom, or for Solomon's Temple.
- No critical year is stated for the birth of Jesus, the crucifixion, Paul's conversion or any stage of his travels.
- Nehemiah's activity is undated; Ezra is "middle of the 5th century BCE" once (guest Higashi, E8).
- The Bar Kokhba revolt is mentioned in passing (E37, E83) and never dated.
- Between 70 CE and 367 CE the show dates texts and canon lists, not events.

### Internal inconsistencies to resolve before a timeline quotes the show

- Fall of Jerusalem: 587 and 586 BCE are both used, sometimes in the same Episode.
- Deutero-Isaiah: "exilic" (E113), "post-exilic" (E104), "around 520 BCE" (E43), "late 6th or 5th century" (E90, E182).
- 1 Enoch: Hellenistic or Greco-Roman, from c. 300 BCE, in most Episodes; Persian period in E187.
- Start of the Hasmonean kingdom: 165 BCE (E71), 160 BCE (E123), 141 BCE (E158).
- Onias III killed: 171 BCE (E32), 170 BCE (E34).
- Covenant Code: 8th-7th century (E93, E184) against "maybe 10th, more likely 9th or 8th" (E21).
- Holiness Code: post-exilic (E64, E114, E186) against a reported view that it is the earliest tithe law (E76).
- Luke: 80-85 CE, 95-105 CE and early 2nd century are all voiced; McClellan's own lean moves later over time.
- Who destroyed Samaria: Tiglath-Pileser III (E64) against Shalmaneser V or Sargon II (E72).
- Uzziah's reign differs slightly between E120 and E133.
- Likely slips: Zephaniah at "the beginning of the 500s BCE ... around the time of Josiah" (E174); the Jerusalem Council in "the 30s CE" (E80), which sits oddly with Paul being active in the 50s-60s; the conquest "almost a thousand years" before Josiah (E46).

## Implications for the map

- A dated claim on the show is nearly always a critical date or range. A hedge is common ("probably", "somewhere around"), and so is a relative date ("decades after Paul's death"). The timeline's data shape needs ranges, relative anchors and attribution to a Speaker.
- The show rarely supplies the traditional half. If the timeline must show both, the traditional date needs an external source for most rows, which fits the map's "Transcript triggers, research corroborates" rule but roughly doubles the sourcing work.
- Authorship is the show's usual route into dating a book. A timeline row for a book may need a "traditional author" field alongside "traditional date".
- A historicity verdict ("did not happen as described") is the critical answer for several events (Exodus, conquest, united monarchy, patriarchs). The timeline needs a way to show an event with a traditional date and no critical one.
